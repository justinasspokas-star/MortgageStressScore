Payment stress

Can’t afford your mortgage?

When the monthly payment no longer fits your income, it can feel like there is only one bad choice. In reality, there may be several possible paths. The right path depends on how late you are, your equity, your loan type, and whether the hardship is temporary or long-term.

Act early: The earlier you compare options, the more control you usually have. This page is educational and does not replace advice from your servicer, a housing counselor, attorney, or licensed professional.

Temporary problem or long-term problem?

Start by separating the cause of stress into two groups.

Temporary hardship

Examples: short-term job gap, temporary medical bills, delayed bonus, temporary family emergency. Options like forbearance or repayment plans may fit better.

Long-term affordability problem

Examples: permanent income reduction, large tax/insurance increase, divorce, unaffordable loan reset. Modification, selling, renting, or deeper budget changes may need to be considered.

Possible next options

  • Contact your servicer: Ask about hardship assistance and timelines.
  • HUD-approved counseling: A counselor may help you understand options and communicate with your servicer.
  • Loan modification: May adjust loan terms if you qualify.
  • Forbearance: Can provide short-term relief but is not debt forgiveness.
  • Refinance: Useful only if the math and qualification make sense.
  • Sell or rent: If the payment is structurally unaffordable, protecting equity may become the priority.

Warning signs you should not wait

  • You are already 30+ days late.
  • You are using credit cards or loans to make the mortgage payment.
  • Your payment is more than 40% of gross monthly income.
  • You received formal default or foreclosure-related letters.
  • Your hardship is likely to last more than 3 months.
