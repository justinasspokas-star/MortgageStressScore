Can't afford your mortgage?

Answer first: If you cannot afford your mortgage, first identify whether the problem is temporary or long-term. Then contact your mortgage servicer, ask about hardship options, consider a HUD-approved housing counselor, and compare repayment plan, forbearance, deferral, loan modification, refinance, selling, or renting options before the situation becomes more expensive.

When the monthly payment no longer fits your income, it can feel like there is only one bad choice. In reality, there may be several possible paths. The right path depends on how late you are, your equity, your loan type, your servicer’s rules, and whether the hardship is temporary or long-term.

Act early: The earlier you compare options, the more control you usually have. This page is educational and does not replace advice from your servicer, a housing counselor, attorney, financial professional, or licensed professional.

Temporary problem or long-term problem?

Start by separating the cause of stress into two groups. This is one of the most important decisions because temporary problems and long-term affordability problems usually need different solutions. If the hardship is short-term, you may need time. If the payment is structurally unaffordable, you may need a deeper change.

Temporary hardship

Examples include a short-term job gap, temporary medical bills, delayed bonus, emergency car repair, or temporary family emergency. Options like forbearance, repayment plans, deferral, or short-term budget changes may fit better.

Long-term affordability problem

Examples include permanent income reduction, large tax or insurance increase, divorce, unaffordable loan reset, or a payment that no longer fits household income. Modification, selling, renting, or deeper budget changes may need to be considered.

Step 1: Know exactly where you stand

Before choosing an option, write down your mortgage payment, full housing cost, monthly income, other debts, cash savings, missed payments, and expected income changes. If your payment is high but you are still current, your goal is to prevent missed payments. If you already missed payments, your goal is to understand deadlines and available loss-mitigation options.

Do not rely on memory. Put the numbers on paper. A clear budget makes every conversation with your servicer, counselor, attorney, or financial professional easier. It also helps you understand whether you need a short-term bridge or a long-term solution.

Step 2: Contact your servicer

Your servicer is the company that collects your mortgage payment. It may not be the original lender. Ask about hardship assistance, timelines, documents, and whether there are options before foreclosure risk grows. You can use a simple sentence: “I am experiencing financial hardship and I want to understand what options are available for my loan.”

Ask for information in writing. Save letters, emails, screenshots, names, dates, and reference numbers. If a representative says something important, ask them to send it in writing or point you to the written policy.

Possible next options

  • Contact your servicer: Ask about hardship assistance, deadlines, required documents, and what happens if you miss another payment.
  • HUD-approved counseling: A counselor may help you understand options and communicate with your servicer.
  • Repayment plan: May help you catch up missed payments over time if income has recovered.
  • Forbearance: Can provide short-term relief, but it is usually not debt forgiveness.
  • Deferral: May move missed amounts to a later date, depending on the program and loan rules.
  • Loan modification: May adjust loan terms if you qualify after hardship review.
  • Refinance: Useful only if the math and qualification make sense.
  • Sell or rent: If the payment is structurally unaffordable, protecting equity may become the priority.

Warning signs that the problem is urgent

Your situation may need immediate attention if you have already missed multiple payments, received formal notices, used credit cards for basic bills, cannot explain how you will make next month’s payment, or are considering paying a company that promises guaranteed relief. These are signs to speak with your servicer and consider a HUD-approved counselor quickly.

What not to do

  • Do not ignore servicer letters or deadlines.
  • Do not pay upfront fees to anyone promising guaranteed modification or foreclosure rescue.
  • Do not assume refinancing is possible if income, credit, or equity has changed.
  • Do not transfer title or sign documents you do not understand.
  • Do not wait until the payment problem becomes a legal emergency.
  • Do not stop communicating because the situation feels embarrassing or stressful.

When selling or renting may be part of the discussion

If the mortgage is permanently unaffordable, keeping the home at all costs may not be the safest financial decision. Selling, renting a room, renting the property, or moving to a lower-cost housing situation can be emotionally difficult, but sometimes protecting equity is better than waiting until options narrow. This decision should be made carefully and with professional guidance when needed.

How to prepare before calling

Before calling your servicer, gather your loan number, recent income information, monthly budget, hardship reason, current payment status, and any documents showing the hardship. Write down your goal before the call. For example: “I need to understand options to avoid missing another payment,” or “I already missed a payment and want to apply for available hardship assistance.”

FAQ

What should I do first if I can’t afford my mortgage?

Write down your full housing cost and income, then contact your servicer to ask about hardship options and deadlines.

Can I ask for help if I am not late yet?

Yes. It is often better to ask early, before missed payments limit your choices or create extra fees and deadlines.

Is loan modification better than refinance?

It depends. Refinance is usually a new loan and requires qualification. Modification is usually a hardship review through your current servicer.

Should I talk to a HUD-approved housing counselor?

It can be useful, especially if you are behind, confused about servicer options, or worried about scams.

Can I lose my home after missing one payment?

One missed payment does not automatically mean foreclosure, but it is a serious warning sign. Contact your servicer and understand the timeline for your loan.
