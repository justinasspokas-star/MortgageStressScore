Mortgage help

Need help with your mortgage payment?

If your mortgage payment is becoming hard to manage, the most important thing is to act early. This guide explains practical next steps before the situation becomes more expensive or harder to fix.

Important: This page is educational only. It is not legal, financial, lending, or foreclosure-prevention advice. If you are behind or close to missing a payment, contact your mortgage servicer and consider speaking with a HUD-approved housing counselor.

First steps to take this week

  1. Check your full monthly housing cost. Include mortgage principal, interest, property taxes, homeowners insurance, HOA fees, and utilities.
  2. Call your mortgage servicer early. Ask what hardship options may be available before you miss more payments.
  3. Write down the reason for hardship. Examples include job loss, medical bills, divorce, insurance increase, tax increase, or reduced income.
  4. Do not ignore letters or calls from your servicer. Missing deadlines can reduce your options.
  5. Avoid upfront-fee rescue offers. Be careful with anyone promising guaranteed mortgage relief.

Options that may be available

Repayment plan

A way to catch up missed payments over time. This may help if your hardship was temporary.

Forbearance

A temporary pause or reduction in payments. It can help short-term, but the missed amount still has to be handled later.

Loan modification

A change to loan terms that may make payments more manageable if the servicer approves the request.

Refinance

May help if you qualify for better terms, but it may not be right if your credit, equity, or income has changed.

Questions to ask your servicer

  • What hardship assistance options are available for my loan type?
  • Will this option affect my credit report?
  • What happens after a forbearance ends?
  • Are there fees, deadlines, or documents I need to provide?
  • Can you send the options in writing?
