Mortgage hardship letter

A mortgage hardship letter explains why you are struggling, what changed financially, and what kind of help you are requesting. It should be clear, honest, and specific.

Last reviewed: May 2026 · About this site

Reminder: A hardship letter does not guarantee approval. Your servicer may require specific forms, documents, and review steps.

What to include

  1. Your loan and contact details. Include your name, property address, and loan number if available.
  2. The hardship reason. Explain what happened: job loss, reduced hours, medical bills, divorce, death in family, tax/insurance increase, or another event.
  3. When the hardship started. Give dates or approximate timing.
  4. Your current income situation. Explain whether the problem is temporary or ongoing.
  5. What you are requesting. Ask to be reviewed for available hardship assistance, forbearance, repayment plan, deferral, or loan modification.
  6. Supporting documents. Mention pay stubs, unemployment proof, medical bills, bank statements, divorce documents, or other documents if relevant.

Simple hardship letter structure

Opening: "I am writing to request a review for mortgage assistance because I am experiencing financial hardship."

Explanation: Describe what changed and how it affected your ability to pay.

Plan: Explain whether you expect the hardship to improve and what payment you may be able to manage.

Request: Ask the servicer to review you for available options and send requirements in writing.

Tips

  • Keep it factual and professional.
  • Do not exaggerate or hide important facts.
  • Ask for written confirmation of next steps.
  • Keep copies of everything you send.
  • Consider speaking with a HUD-approved housing counselor if you are unsure.
