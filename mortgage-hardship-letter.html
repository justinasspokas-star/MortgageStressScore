Mortgage hardship letter
free template and examples.

A mortgage hardship letter should be short, factual, and matched to your documents. Use it to explain what changed financially and to request review for all available servicer options.

Hardship letter templateLoan modification letterForbearance requestServicer document packet
Last reviewed: August 2026Reading time: 7–9 minutesEducational template — not legal advice
Quick answer

A strong mortgage hardship letter has five parts: who you are, what happened, when it started, whether the hardship is temporary or long-term, and what help you are asking for. It should not exaggerate, blame, or promise payments you cannot afford.

Free mortgage hardship letter template

Copy and customize this template
[Your name] [Property address] [Loan number] [Phone and email] [Date] To the Loss Mitigation Department, I am writing to request a review for mortgage hardship assistance on my loan. I am experiencing financial hardship because of [job loss / reduced hours / medical bills / divorce / death of a household member / disaster damage / escrow or insurance increase / other reason]. This hardship began around [month and year]. Before the hardship, my gross monthly income was approximately $[amount]. My current gross monthly income is approximately $[amount]. My regular mortgage payment is $[amount], and I am currently [current / one payment behind / two or more payments behind / facing a foreclosure notice]. I believe this hardship is [temporary and expected to improve by ___ / long-term and I need a more permanent payment solution]. I am committed to resolving the situation and keeping the home if the payment can be made sustainable. Please review me for all available mortgage hardship assistance options for my loan, including repayment plan, forbearance, payment deferral or partial claim if available, and loan modification. Please tell me in writing what documents are required, whether my application is complete, and the deadline for any missing items. I am attaching the following documents: [list pay stubs, bank statements, unemployment letter, medical bills, divorce decree, death certificate, insurance/tax notice, or other proof]. Thank you, [Your signature] [Your printed name]

Examples by hardship type

Job loss“I lost my job on [date]. I am actively seeking work and currently receiving/expecting unemployment income.”Termination letter, unemployment notice, bank statements, job-offer status.
Reduced hours“My employer reduced my hours from [old schedule] to [new schedule], reducing my monthly income from $X to $Y.”Pay stubs before/after, employer letter, bank statements.
Medical hardship“A medical issue increased expenses and reduced my ability to work/pay the mortgage.”Medical bills, insurance statements, doctor/work restrictions, income proof.
Divorce or separation“The household income changed after separation/divorce, and I am requesting review based on my current income.”Divorce filing/decree, support order, current income, bank statements.
Escrow, tax, or insurance increase“My payment increased because of escrow/taxes/insurance, and the new monthly payment is not sustainable.”Escrow analysis, tax bill, insurance premium notice, current budget.
Disaster damage“Property damage or local disaster costs created a temporary payment hardship.”Insurance claim, FEMA/local notice, repair estimates, photos, income proof.

What to include

  1. Loan details: full name, property address, loan number, and best contact information.
  2. Hardship reason: one specific reason or primary event. Keep it factual.
  3. Timeline: when it started, whether it is still happening, and when you expect things to improve.
  4. Numbers: old income, current income, monthly payment, and past-due amount if known.
  5. Request: ask for review for all available loss-mitigation options, not only one product.
  6. Attachments: list every document you are sending so nothing looks random or unsupported.

Mistakes that weaken the letter

Avoid these common mistakes
  • Writing a long emotional story without numbers or dates.
  • Promising a repayment amount you cannot actually afford.
  • Asking only for “a modification” when forbearance, repayment, partial claim, or deferral might also apply.
  • Sending documents without keeping a copy or upload confirmation.
  • Paying a third-party company upfront to “guarantee” mortgage relief.

How to send and follow up

Send the letter through the servicer’s official upload portal if available. If you mail it, use a trackable method. After submitting, ask the servicer whether the package is complete, what is missing, and what deadline applies.

Follow-up script

“I submitted my mortgage hardship package on [date]. Please confirm whether it is complete or incomplete. If anything is missing, please tell me the exact document, acceptable format, upload location, and deadline in writing.”

CFPB Regulation X says that if a servicer receives a loss-mitigation application 45 days or more before a foreclosure sale, the servicer must review it promptly and notify the borrower in writing within 5 days, excluding legal public holidays, Saturdays, and Sundays, whether the application is complete or incomplete.

When to ask a HUD counselor for help

If you are confused, already behind, or facing a foreclosure notice, a HUD-approved housing counselor can help you organize documents, understand options, and prepare for calls with your servicer. CFPB recommends calling your servicer right away if you cannot pay or are worried about missing a payment, and says a HUD-approved counselor can help if you do not know your servicer or need guidance.
