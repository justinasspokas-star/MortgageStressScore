Refinance vs loan modification

Refinancing and loan modification both change your mortgage situation, but they are very different tools. One is usually a new loan. The other is usually a change to your existing loan through your servicer.

Simple difference: A refinance often depends on credit, income, equity, rates, and closing costs. A loan modification usually depends on hardship review and servicer/investor rules.

When refinancing may fit

  • You are current on payments.
  • You have enough income to qualify.
  • Your credit profile is still strong enough.
  • You have equity or meet the lender's requirements.
  • The new loan saves money after closing costs.

When loan modification may fit

  • You have a documented hardship.
  • You may not qualify for a refinance.
  • You are already behind or at risk of falling behind.
  • You need a payment review through your current servicer.
  • You want to explore loss-mitigation options before foreclosure risk grows.

Key comparison

Refinance

Best for: borrowers who can qualify for a new loan and benefit from better terms.

Watch out for: closing costs, higher total interest, points, and restarting the loan term.

Loan modification

Best for: hardship situations where the existing loan needs adjustment.

Watch out for: documentation requirements, approval uncertainty, and credit/reporting effects.

Questions before choosing

  1. Am I current, late, or at risk of becoming late?
  2. Do I have enough income and credit to refinance?
  3. How long will I stay in the home?
  4. What is the break-even period after closing costs?
  5. Has my servicer explained modification or hardship options in writing?
