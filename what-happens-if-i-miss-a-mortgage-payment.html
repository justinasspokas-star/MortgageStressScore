What happens if I miss a mortgage payment?

Answer first: If you miss a mortgage payment, you may face a late fee, servicer contact, credit reporting if the payment remains unpaid long enough, and eventually more serious collection or foreclosure-related steps if the missed payments continue. One missed payment does not automatically mean foreclosure, but it is a warning sign. Contact your servicer quickly, ask about options, and keep every notice and deadline in writing.

Missing a mortgage payment can feel frightening, especially if it has never happened before. The exact timeline depends on your loan, servicer, state law, grace period, payment history, and investor rules. Still, there are general patterns that homeowners should understand so they can act early instead of waiting until the situation becomes more expensive.

General timeline after a missed mortgage payment

Every mortgage is different, but the following timeline explains what many homeowners may experience. Your actual deadlines can be different, so always check your mortgage documents and servicer notices.

Days 1–15: grace period may apply

Many mortgages have a short grace period after the due date. During this period, the payment is late technically, but a late fee may not apply yet. This does not mean you should ignore it. If you already know you cannot pay, contact your servicer before the situation grows.

After the grace period: late fee may be charged

If the payment remains unpaid after the grace period, your servicer may charge a late fee. The amount depends on your loan documents and applicable rules. You may also begin receiving reminders, calls, emails, or letters from the servicer.

Around 30 days late: credit reporting may become a concern

If the payment remains unpaid long enough, it may be reported as delinquent to credit bureaus. Credit reporting rules and timing can vary, but this is one reason to act quickly. If you can pay before a reporting threshold, ask your servicer how timing works for your loan.

Multiple missed payments: options become more urgent

After multiple missed payments, the situation can become more serious. You may receive formal notices, document requests, or loss-mitigation information. At this stage, you should not rely on verbal promises. Keep copies of every notice and ask for written instructions.

What to do in the first 7 days

If you missed a payment or know you are about to miss one, create a short action plan. First, confirm the exact amount due and whether a late fee applies. Second, review your next paycheck, savings, and essential expenses. Third, call your servicer and ask what options exist if you cannot catch up immediately.

  1. Log in to your servicer portal. Check amount due, due date, fees, escrow, and notices.
  2. Call your servicer. Ask what options are available and what deadlines matter.
  3. Write down the hardship reason. Job loss, medical bills, reduced hours, divorce, tax increase, or insurance increase.
  4. Prepare basic documents. Pay stubs, bank statements, unemployment proof, medical bills, or hardship explanation.
  5. Avoid scams. Do not pay upfront-fee companies promising guaranteed mortgage relief.

Options your servicer may discuss

The available options depend on your loan type, servicer, investor rules, hardship reason, and how far behind you are. Common categories include repayment plan, forbearance, deferral, and loan modification.

Repayment plan

You repay missed amounts over time by adding extra to future payments. It may fit if the hardship has ended and income recovered.

Forbearance

Temporary payment pause or reduction. It may help during short-term hardship, but missed amounts usually must be handled later.

Deferral

Some missed amounts may be moved to a later date, depending on your loan and program rules.

Loan modification

The existing mortgage terms may be reviewed and adjusted if you qualify under hardship rules.

Questions to ask your servicer

  • How many days late is my account right now?
  • Has a late fee been charged?
  • When could this be reported to credit bureaus?
  • What loss-mitigation or hardship options are available for my loan?
  • What documents do I need to submit?
  • What deadlines should I know?
  • Will any option require a trial payment plan?
  • Can you send the requirements in writing?

What not to do after missing a payment

Do not ignore notices. Do not assume one missed payment will solve itself next month if your budget still does not work. Do not pay a company that promises guaranteed modification or foreclosure rescue. Do not transfer title, sign documents you do not understand, or stop talking to your servicer because a third party told you to.

When to get extra help

If you missed more than one payment, received formal notices, cannot understand the servicer’s instructions, or feel pressured by a rescue company, consider contacting a HUD-approved housing counselor. If you have legal documents or foreclosure notices, consider speaking with a qualified attorney in your state.

FAQ

Will missing one mortgage payment ruin my credit?

One missed payment can become a credit issue if it remains unpaid long enough to be reported. Ask your servicer how timing works for your account and act quickly.

Can I avoid a late fee?

If you pay within the grace period, a late fee may not apply. Check your loan documents and servicer portal because grace periods can vary.

Should I call my servicer before I miss the payment?

Yes. If you know you cannot pay on time, calling early can help you understand available options before the account becomes more delinquent.

Do I have to pay all missed payments at once?

Not always. Depending on your loan and approval, options may include repayment plan, deferral, forbearance exit options, or loan modification.

Can foreclosure start after one missed payment?

One missed payment does not automatically mean foreclosure, but timelines vary and notices matter. Do not ignore servicer communications.
