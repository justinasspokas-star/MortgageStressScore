California mortgage assistance: CalAssist deadline and free help

Looking for mortgage payment assistance in California? CalAssist is accepting applications through November 2, 2026 for eligible homeowners whose primary residence was destroyed or made uninhabitable by a qualifying California disaster. It is not general mortgage hardship aid. Other homeowners can still ask their servicer about loss mitigation, free HUD-approved housing counseling and California Homeowner Bill of Rights protections.

CalAssist Mortgage Fund: who can apply?

The California Housing Finance Agency administers CalAssist to help owners of eligible homes affected by declared disasters. The program advertises up to one year of eligible mortgage payments and a possible grant of up to $100,000, subject to limits and review. Grants are not loans and do not require repayment, but not every homeowner qualifies.

  • Disaster qualification: the affected home must have been destroyed or so severely damaged that it was deemed uninhabitable because of a qualifying disaster between January 1, 2023 and January 8, 2025.
  • Primary residence: the damaged property must have been the applicant's primary residence immediately before the qualifying disaster, with other property and occupancy conditions applying.
  • Income: combined income of people named on the mortgage or deed must fall within the applicable county limits.
  • Mortgage type: program rules address eligible mortgages, including some reverse mortgages and HELOCs. Your loan and participating servicer must meet current requirements.
  • Not general hardship funding: being behind on payments, unemployed or affected by rising escrow costs alone does not establish CalAssist disaster eligibility.

Confirm your specific event against the official list of qualifying disasters, including the 2025 Eaton and Palisades fires where applicable. Check the participating servicer list before relying on the grant.

How to apply before November 2, 2026

  1. Check eligibility first at the official CalAssist site. Confirm the disaster, property and household income rules.
  2. Prepare documentation: mortgage statement, proof of income, bank statements, a pre-disaster utility bill and any additional documents requested during review.
  3. Complete and submit the application before November 2. A saved but incomplete draft does not meet the stated submission deadline.
  4. Monitor your email for requests for additional information. Review may take several weeks; applications submitted on time can continue to be processed after the deadline.
  5. Check the mortgage servicer: assistance is paid directly to participating servicers, so ask about participation if yours is not listed.

Official contact: CalAssist Contact Center 800-501-0019, Monday–Friday, 8 a.m.–5 p.m. The official site provides application help and free counseling referrals. Current CalAssist details →

What if you do not qualify for CalAssist?

Call the company that services your mortgage and explain the payment difficulty. Ask to be considered for options permitted by your loan type, such as forbearance, a repayment plan or a modification. Do not stop making payments or wait for grants based on generic online promises. If you already have a recorded Notice of Default or scheduled sale, seek free housing counseling and qualified legal help immediately.

California Homeowner Bill of Rights: practical protections

California's Homeowner Bill of Rights includes servicer communication requirements and, in qualifying circumstances, restrictions on dual tracking—advancing foreclosure while a complete loan-modification application is under review. These restrictions are conditional: whether they apply depends on the borrower, loan, timing, completeness and current legal requirements. An application does not automatically stop every foreclosure sale.

  • Ask in writing what documents are missing, whether your application is complete and which deadlines apply.
  • Save proof of submission and every response; note any Notice of Default or trustee-sale date.
  • Ask your servicer whether the foreclosure process is paused and on what legal or contractual basis. Never assume a pause without confirmation.
  • Contact an attorney promptly if you suspect a violation or a sale is imminent. California Courts publishes guidance for homeowners.

Official explanations: California Attorney General — Homeowner Bill of Rights and California Courts — nonjudicial foreclosures.

How foreclosure works in California

Many California residential foreclosures use a nonjudicial trustee-sale procedure. A notice of default, a waiting period, and a notice of sale are typically required; exact protections and rights depend on the circumstances. California Courts explains the ordinary Notice of Default stage, the roughly 90-day statutory period before a Notice of Sale, and a minimum notice period before a sale. Do not treat general timelines as a deadline for your specific home.

If you receive a Notice of Default or Notice of Sale, read it immediately and contact a housing counselor or attorney. Consult the California Courts self-help page for the current process and homeowner rights.

California mortgage assistance: common questions

Can any California homeowner receive $100,000?

No. CalAssist is for eligible qualifying disaster-related damage to a primary residence. The advertised amount is a maximum, not a guaranteed grant.

What is the CalAssist application deadline?

According to its official website as checked October 19, 2026, the deadline for completed submissions is November 2, 2026.

Does a mortgage modification request automatically stop a trustee sale?

No. Some borrowers have legal protections when a timely, complete application meets specific requirements, but you must confirm your case's status and seek legal assistance if a sale is scheduled.
