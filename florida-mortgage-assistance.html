Florida mortgage assistance programs in 2026

If you are struggling with mortgage payments in Florida, act early. Florida generally uses judicial foreclosure, but court deadlines can be short. Here are practical next steps: contact your servicer, consider free HUD-approved counseling, verify county programs and seek legal aid when needed.

Last reviewed: October 28, 2026 · About this site

Florida generally uses judicial foreclosure: A lender must proceed through court. There is no guaranteed 8–36 month waiting period. Do not wait for a lawsuit before asking for help. If served, check your summons and contact legal aid or an attorney immediately.

How Florida judicial foreclosure actually works

Understanding the process is the first step to protecting yourself. Florida foreclosure proceeds through the circuit court of the county where your property is located. Here is what the timeline typically looks like:

  • Days 1–120 of delinquency: For many loans, federal servicing rules generally prohibit starting foreclosure until the borrower is more than 120 days overdue, with exceptions. Contact your servicer as soon as you anticipate difficulty.
  • Lender files lawsuit: After applicable requirements are met, a lender may file a foreclosure complaint in circuit court. Service of a summons and complaint starts important response deadlines.
  • You have 20 days to respond: Florida defendants generally have 20 days after service to respond. Verify the exact deadline on your summons and get legal help quickly. Ignoring court papers can lead to default.
  • Discovery and hearings: If you file an answer, both sides exchange information and attend hearings. This phase can last months to years depending on complexity and court docket.
  • Summary judgment or trial: The lender must prove its case to the court. Homeowners with defenses can contest this step, further extending the timeline.
  • Foreclosure sale: If the lender wins, the court sets a sale date. Florida foreclosure sales are conducted online through the county clerk's auction platform.
  • Right of redemption: Under Florida Statutes §45.0315, redemption generally ends at the later of filing the certificate of sale or the time set out in the judgment or order. Check the deadline in your case with a Florida attorney.
Respond to the summons: Filing an Answer to a foreclosure lawsuit does not mean you are "fighting the bank." It simply preserves your rights and gives you time to pursue modifications, short sales, or other resolutions. Homeowners who ignore the summons routinely lose options they would have had.

Florida homeowner assistance programs in 2026

Florida Homeowner Assistance Fund (HAF)

Florida's Homeowner Assistance Fund — administered through the Florida Department of Economic Opportunity (DEO) — provided assistance to eligible homeowners for past-due mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, HOA fees, and utilities. Past program benefits do not mean new assistance is available.

HAF is closed to new assistance. Treasury confirms the Homeowner Assistance Fund award period ended on September 30, 2026. HAF recipients can no longer use those funds to provide homeowner assistance. Florida also closed its HAF applications earlier. Do not rely on a new HAF grant. Official Treasury HAF status.

Florida Housing Finance Corporation — Foreclosure Prevention Resources

Florida Housing Finance Corporation lists foreclosure-prevention resources and HUD-approved housing counseling resources. These counselors help homeowners understand servicer options, prepare for modification applications, and navigate the court process. Access the directory at floridahousing.org.

HUD-approved housing counselors (free)

HUD-approved foreclosure-prevention counseling is free. Counselors can help prepare documents, contact servicers and explain relief options. Counseling alone does not automatically postpone a foreclosure. Find a counselor at hud.gov/housingcounseling.

Miami-Dade Mortgage Relief Program

Miami-Dade County lists a separate Mortgage Relief Program with up to $3,500 for eligible households toward certain overdue housing costs. This is not the closed federal HAF. Check current applications and income requirements with Miami-Dade County before relying on funds.

Legal resources for Florida homeowners in foreclosure

Because Florida foreclosure requires a court process, legal representation — or at minimum, understanding your legal rights — is more valuable here than in non-judicial states.

  • Florida Bar Lawyer Referral Service: 1-800-342-8011. Connects homeowners with attorneys who handle foreclosure defense. Many offer free or low-cost initial consultations.
  • Bay Area Legal Services (Tampa): Free legal help for low-income homeowners in Hillsborough, Pasco, Pinellas, Manatee, and Sarasota counties.
  • Legal Aid Society of Broward County: Free foreclosure defense for eligible low-income residents in Broward County.
  • Three Rivers Legal Services (Gainesville area): Foreclosure prevention legal assistance for 23 north Florida counties.
  • Florida Rural Legal Services: Covers rural and central Florida counties with free legal assistance for homeowners.

Servicer loss-mitigation — your primary option

Regardless of what state or local programs are available, your mortgage servicer's loss-mitigation programs are the most direct path to keeping your home. Court timelines vary. Apply early, and do not assume an application automatically stops a scheduled sale.

Contact your servicer before or immediately after the first missed payment. Use specific language: "I want to be reviewed for loss-mitigation options." If you have an FHA loan, reference your rights under HUD guidelines. See our mortgage payment help guide for how to prepare for the servicer conversation.

Deficiency judgments: what Florida homeowners need to know

Unlike some states that prohibit deficiency judgments after foreclosure, Florida allows lenders to pursue the difference between the foreclosure sale price and the remaining loan balance. The lender has one year from the foreclosure sale date to file for a deficiency judgment. If you are considering a short sale or deed in lieu of foreclosure, negotiating a written waiver of deficiency from the lender is important — do not assume it is automatic. A Florida attorney or HUD counselor can help you negotiate this term before agreeing to any resolution.
