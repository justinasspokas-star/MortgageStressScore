How to apply for an FHA loan modification

If your FHA mortgage payment is difficult to manage, start with your mortgage servicer. This checklist explains what to ask, what documents to prepare, how the review works and what to do when you receive an answer. For a comparison of FHA programs, see our FHA loss-mitigation options guide.

Last reviewed: October 19, 2026 · Editorial policy and sources

First step: Call the mortgage servicer named on your statement. Ask to be evaluated for all currently available FHA loss-mitigation options. You do not need to decide in advance which option you qualify for. Servicer review and FHA rules determine eligibility.

Before you apply: identify your loan and hardship

Check your mortgage statement or closing documents to confirm your loan is FHA-insured. Explain whether your difficulty is temporary, ongoing, or related to changes in income, expenses, taxes or insurance. If you are already behind, tell your servicer how many payments you have missed and ask about any urgent deadlines.

FHA loan modification documents checklist

  • Most recent mortgage statement and servicer account number.
  • Current income information, such as pay stubs, benefit statements or self-employment documents, if requested.
  • Monthly expenses and household budget, including property taxes and insurance.
  • Details of the hardship and when it started; a hardship letter if requested.
  • Bank statements or other documents identified by your servicer. Ask for a written list; requirements are not identical for every file.
  • Copies of submitted documents and confirmation of each submission date.

How to request a modification review: five steps

  1. Call or message your servicer. Ask for the FHA loss-mitigation department and request a review of every currently available home-retention option.
  2. Get the application and deadlines. Ask what documents are required, how to submit them securely and when you should expect an update.
  3. Submit a complete package. Keep copies and request confirmation that no documents are missing.
  4. Compare the written offer. Check the new payment, interest rate, mortgage term, escrow charges, any partial-claim lien and whether a trial payment plan is required.
  5. Confirm the outcome. If approved, follow the written acceptance and payment instructions. If denied, request the specific reasons and ask whether other options were considered.
Servicer call script: “I have an FHA-insured mortgage and am experiencing a payment hardship. Please evaluate me for all currently available FHA home-retention and loss-mitigation options. What documents do you need, what are the deadlines, and when can I expect a written decision?”

Modification, partial claim or Payment Supplement?

A loan modification changes mortgage terms; a partial claim may move eligible past-due amounts into a separate, typically interest-free subordinate lien; a Payment Supplement may temporarily reduce payments for eligible FHA borrowers. These options have distinct requirements and consequences, and not all borrowers qualify for all options.

Read the detailed FHA modification options comparison for the current program descriptions and questions to ask.

What if your FHA modification is denied?

Ask the servicer for the written explanation and whether other FHA options were considered. Request information on any applicable appeals or deadlines. A HUD-approved housing counselor can help you review the answer at no charge. If you think the servicer handled the application incorrectly, see our mortgage servicer complaint guide.

How long does an FHA modification take?

There is no reliable universal approval time. Timing depends on when your servicer receives a complete file, the type of assistance considered, trial payments (if required), applicable foreclosure deadlines and borrower circumstances. Ask for your specific written deadlines rather than relying on generic 30–90-day estimates.
