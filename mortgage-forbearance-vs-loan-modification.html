Mortgage forbearance vs loan modification

Forbearance and loan modification are both hardship-related mortgage options, but they solve different problems. Forbearance is usually temporary relief. A loan modification is usually a longer-term change to the mortgage terms.

Important: Forbearance does not usually erase the amount you do not pay. Ask your servicer what happens when the forbearance period ends.

What is forbearance?

Forbearance may allow you to temporarily pause or reduce payments during a hardship. It can help if your situation is temporary, but the missed amount must be handled later through a repayment plan, deferral, modification, or another solution depending on your loan and servicer rules.

What is a loan modification?

A loan modification changes the terms of the existing mortgage. Depending on the program and approval, it may adjust interest rate, term length, payment structure, or how past-due amounts are handled.

Side-by-side comparison

Forbearance

  • Short-term relief
  • May help during temporary hardship
  • Requires a plan for missed payments later
  • Ask how credit reporting works

Loan modification

  • Longer-term payment review
  • May fit ongoing hardship
  • Requires documents and approval
  • May affect loan terms and reporting

Ask your servicer these questions

  • Is forbearance available for my loan type?
  • What happens to missed payments after forbearance?
  • Can I be reviewed for modification after or instead of forbearance?
  • What documents do you need?
  • Will you send the plan in writing before I agree?
