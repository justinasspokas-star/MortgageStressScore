Forbearance vs. loan modification

Forbearance is usually temporary relief. A loan modification may create a longer-term change to your mortgage terms. Know the difference before you ask.

Quick comparison

OptionMain purposeImportant caution
ForbearanceTemporarily pause or reduce payments during short-term hardship.Payments are usually not erased; they must be handled later.
Loan modificationAdjust loan terms to create a more sustainable payment after hardship review.Approval is not automatic and documentation is usually required.

Forbearance may make sense when

  • Your hardship is temporary.
  • You expect income to recover.
  • You need short-term breathing room.
  • You understand what happens when forbearance ends.

Loan modification may make sense when

  • The affordability problem is longer term.
  • You cannot catch up quickly.
  • You may not qualify for refinance.
  • You need the servicer to evaluate a sustainable plan.
Urgent: If foreclosure notices or legal deadlines are involved, contact your servicer and consider qualified housing or legal help quickly. This page is educational only.

Before choosing, ask

  1. Will payments be deferred, repaid monthly, or due in a lump sum?
  2. How will this be reported to credit bureaus?
  3. Can I apply for modification after forbearance?
  4. What paperwork is required?
  5. What are the deadlines?
