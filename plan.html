Important: This page is educational only. It is not legal, financial, tax, lending, foreclosure-prevention, or housing counseling advice. If you are behind or close to missing a payment, contact your mortgage servicer and consider speaking with a HUD-approved housing counselor.

Use the steps below to organize your next conversation with your mortgage servicer and keep your documents in one place.

1. Write your hardship reason in one sentence

Keep it factual and short: income reduced, medical expenses, temporary job loss, insurance/tax increase, divorce, death in household, ARM reset, or unexpected repair costs.

2. Gather your basic documents

  • Most recent mortgage statement
  • Recent pay stubs or income documentation
  • Bank statements
  • Tax return or W-2/1099 documents if requested
  • Hardship explanation
  • Monthly budget snapshot
  • Any letters from your servicer

3. Ask your servicer these questions

  • What hardship options are available for my loan?
  • What documents do you need from me?
  • What is the deadline to respond?
  • Will this option affect my credit reporting?
  • What happens after a temporary plan ends?
  • Can you send the available options in writing?

4. Compare options carefully

Do not only ask, “Can I lower the payment?” Ask what happens later. Some options defer payments, some change terms, and some may create a larger balance or future payment change.

5. Avoid urgent-fee scams

Be careful with anyone who guarantees foreclosure rescue, asks for large upfront fees, tells you to stop communicating with your servicer, or pressures you to sign documents quickly.

6. Free official help

You can look for a HUD-approved housing counselor through HUD’s official resources. Counseling may be free or low-cost.
