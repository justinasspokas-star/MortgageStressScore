Quick answer

FHA loan modification is one possible FHA loss-mitigation option, but it is not the only one. Depending on your situation, your servicer may review repayment plans, forbearance, standalone loan modification, standalone partial claim, combination modification and partial claim, or Payment Supplement. Your job is to ask for the full review, send a complete package, and get deadlines and decisions in writing.

Start here if your loan is FHA

An FHA loan is insured by the Federal Housing Administration, but HUD is usually not the company collecting your payment. Your mortgage servicer handles payment collection, delinquency notices, document uploads, and loss-mitigation review. That means the first practical step is to call the servicer and ask for the loss mitigation department.

Do not only ask, “Can I get a modification?” That question is too narrow. A better question is:

Use this exact first question

“My loan is FHA-insured and I am having trouble with my mortgage payment. Please review me for all available FHA home-retention loss-mitigation options, including repayment, forbearance, standalone partial claim, standalone modification, combination modification and partial claim, and Payment Supplement if available. What documents do you need from me, and what is my deadline?”

CFPB explains that a loan modification changes the terms of a mortgage and is a type of loss mitigation. It may reduce the monthly payment, but it can also affect the repayment term and total amount owed, so homeowners should understand both short-term and long-term effects before accepting an offer. Source: CFPB loan modification explanation.

What changed in FHA loss mitigation

FHA loss mitigation has changed since the COVID-era emergency programs. HUD announced updates to FHA’s permanent loss-mitigation framework through Mortgagee Letters and Handbook 4000.1 updates. HUD’s industry notices describe a shift toward updated permanent options, with implementation of revised loss-mitigation policies in 2025 and ongoing guidance for servicers.

SEO-important point: for 2026 content, do not write as if old COVID-19 Recovery Options are the normal path. The page should focus on current FHA home-retention review: repayment/forbearance when appropriate, modification, partial claim, combination modification plus partial claim, and Payment Supplement when available under current HUD rules.

Program details can change. Your servicer’s written review matters more than any general article. Ask for the current FHA option list, the specific reason for approval or denial, and any trial payment plan requirement in writing.

FHA home-retention options compared

Use this table before you call your servicer. It gives you the plain-English meaning of each major option and the question to ask. The servicer decides eligibility under FHA rules and your file, not this website.

Repayment planYou keep your normal payment and add extra money each month until the missed amount is caught up.Short delinquency and enough income to afford more than the regular payment.“What would my total monthly payment be under the plan, and how many months would it last?”
ForbearanceA temporary pause or reduction in payments while a short-term hardship is resolved.Temporary income loss, illness, disaster, or short setback.“How will the paused payments be repaid when forbearance ends?”

How to talk about the FHA “waterfall”

Servicers often use the word waterfall for the order in which options are reviewed. You do not need to know every technical calculation, but you do need to make sure your file is reviewed under the correct FHA process.

Say this:

Servicer script

“Please tell me where I am in the FHA loss-mitigation waterfall. Which options have already been reviewed? Which options were not reviewed, and why? If a trial payment plan is required, please send the exact payment amount, due dates, and what happens after successful completion.”

HUD’s 2025 FHA notices describe new permanent loss-mitigation options, a move away from the COVID-era waterfall, and updates around repayment, forbearance, home-retention options, trial payment plans, partial claims, modifications, and Payment Supplement. Source: HUD FHA INFO updates.

How urgent is this?

If you are already behind, do not wait for the next letter. CFPB says that, except in rare cases, a servicer generally cannot start the foreclosure process until at least 120 days after delinquency. But that does not mean you should wait 120 days. Your state timeline, your servicer’s letters, and any sale date matter.

Your situationRisk levelWhat to do now
Not late yet, but next payment looks impossibleEarly warningCall before missing the payment. Ask what FHA hardship options exist before delinquency starts.
1 missed paymentAct this weekAsk for the loss-mitigation package and document upload instructions.
2–3 missed paymentsUrgentAsk for all FHA home-retention options and get the deadline for a complete package in writing.
4+ missed payments or foreclosure lettersCriticalCall servicer, contact a HUD-approved counselor, and consider legal aid if court papers or a sale date exist.

For foreclosure-prevention help, HUD-approved housing counseling agencies are a key official resource. HUD says foreclosure, eviction, and homeless counseling are always free through participating housing counseling agencies. Source: HUD housing counseling.

Documents to prepare before you apply

A “complete package” matters. If documents are missing, the review can stall. Prepare digital copies before you call.

  • Latest mortgage statement and any delinquency or foreclosure letters.
  • Proof of income: pay stubs, benefits letters, pension, unemployment, self-employment profit/loss, or other income documentation.
  • Recent bank statements.
  • Hardship explanation: job loss, medical issue, divorce, income drop, death in household, payment shock, disaster, or other reason.
  • Monthly budget showing what payment you can realistically afford.
  • Tax returns or IRS transcripts if requested.
  • Any homeowner association, property tax, or insurance information if payment stress involves escrow or housing costs.

Important: if your servicer says something is missing, ask them to identify the exact document by name, how to submit it, and the deadline. Keep screenshots, upload confirmations, fax receipts, email confirmations, and certified-mail tracking.

Questions to ask your FHA servicer

Copy these questions into a note before calling. The goal is to control the conversation and leave with exact next steps.

  1. Confirm FHA status. “Is my loan currently FHA-insured? Can you confirm the FHA case number or where I can find it?”
  2. Ask for the exact arrears. “What is the current delinquency amount, reinstatement amount, and next deadline?”
  3. Ask for all home-retention options. “Which FHA loss-mitigation options will you evaluate?”
  4. Ask about Payment Supplement. “Was I reviewed for Payment Supplement? If not, what rule prevents review?”
  5. Ask about partial claim terms. “If a partial claim is offered, when is it due and what lien or subordinate document will be recorded?”
  6. Ask about trial payments. “Will there be a trial payment plan? What happens if I complete it successfully?”
  7. Ask for written reasons. “If I am denied, will you send the exact reason and any appeal or reconsideration options?”
  8. Ask about sale date protection. “Is there a foreclosure sale date? If I submit a complete application, what happens to the sale timeline?”

Common mistakes FHA borrowers make

Only asking for “a mod”

Ask for full FHA loss-mitigation review, not just one option. The servicer may need to evaluate more than one path.

Waiting for a perfect package

Call early even if you are still gathering documents. Ask what is missing and what deadline applies.

Assuming partial claim is forgiveness

It may help solve arrears, but it is usually not free money. Ask when and how it becomes due.

Accepting a payment you cannot afford

A modification only helps if the new payment is sustainable. CFPB warns that if a modified payment is still unaffordable, the home may still be at risk.

If your FHA modification request is denied

A denial does not always mean the story is over. It may mean the file was incomplete, income was not verified, the option did not meet FHA calculations, the home is not owner-occupied, a trial payment plan failed, or the servicer believes a different option applies.

Ask for the denial reason in writing. Then ask:

  • Was my package complete?
  • Which FHA options were reviewed?
  • Was I reviewed for a lower payment or only for arrears resolution?
  • Is there an appeal, escalation, or reconsideration path?
  • What is the next foreclosure deadline?

CFPB says homeowners may need to consult an attorney if they are facing imminent foreclosure or have been served with legal papers. HUD-approved counseling can also help you organize options and communicate with your mortgage company. Sources: CFPB and CFPB housing counselor guide.

Avoid FHA mortgage relief scams

People behind on FHA loans are often targeted by “foreclosure rescue” or “mortgage relief” companies. Be careful with anyone who says they can guarantee a modification, asks for upfront money, tells you to stop talking to your servicer, or asks you to sign over the deed.

Red flags
  • “Pay us first and we will stop foreclosure.”
  • “Do not contact your mortgage company.”
  • “Sign your home over temporarily.”
  • “Guaranteed government modification.”
  • “Send payments to us instead of your servicer.”

FTC warns consumers not to pay a company upfront for promises of mortgage relief and says upfront fees before services or results are a common scam warning sign. Source: FTC mortgage relief scams.

FHA loan modification FAQ

Can FHA loans be modified?

Yes. FHA-insured loans may be reviewed for FHA loss mitigation, including loan modification and other home-retention options. Eligibility depends on your loan, hardship, documents, timing, affordability, and current HUD rules.

Is an FHA partial claim free money?

No. A partial claim is generally a separate subordinate obligation. Ask exactly when it becomes due, whether a lien is recorded, and how it affects a future sale or refinance.

Who do I call for FHA mortgage help?

Call your mortgage servicer and ask for the loss-mitigation department. You can also contact a HUD-approved housing counseling agency. Foreclosure-prevention counseling from HUD-approved agencies is free.

What is the difference between FHA forbearance and FHA loan modification?

Forbearance is temporary payment relief. A loan modification changes mortgage terms and may create a longer-term payment solution. CFPB explains that forbearance does not erase payments; you still need a plan for the paused or reduced amounts.

What if I already have foreclosure papers?

Call your servicer immediately, ask for the foreclosure sale timeline, contact a HUD-approved counselor, and consider legal aid or an attorney if you have been served with legal papers. Do not wait for another notice.
