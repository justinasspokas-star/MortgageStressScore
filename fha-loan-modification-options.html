How FHA loss mitigation works

An FHA loan is insured by the Federal Housing Administration. That does not mean HUD services your loan directly. You still call your mortgage servicer first. But FHA rules give servicers a specific menu of home-retention options to review when an FHA borrower has a financial hardship.

The goal of FHA loss mitigation is to help eligible borrowers keep their homes when the hardship can be resolved with a workable plan. Not every borrower qualifies for every option, and program details can change. That is why your first question should be: “Which FHA loss-mitigation options did you evaluate for my account?”

Common FHA home-retention options

FHA’s current loss-mitigation framework includes several possible tools. The exact sequence and availability depend on current HUD guidance, your delinquency status, your payment, your hardship, and whether you can afford the home going forward.

Partial claim and Payment Supplement: ask clearly

Many FHA borrowers hear the term “partial claim” but do not understand it. In simple terms, a partial claim can move eligible missed amounts into a separate subordinate obligation. It is not the same as forgiveness. It usually becomes due later, often when the home is sold, refinanced, or the first mortgage is paid off.

The FHA Payment Supplement is a newer loss-mitigation option designed to help some struggling FHA borrowers by using a partial claim structure and a temporary supplement to reduce part of the monthly payment. Because the rules are technical, do not guess. Ask the servicer whether you were evaluated for it, whether you are eligible, and why or why not.

Exact question

“For my FHA loan, did you evaluate me for a partial claim, standalone modification, combination modification and partial claim, and Payment Supplement? If not, can you explain the reason in writing?”

Step-by-step FHA action plan

  1. Confirm your loan is FHA-insured. Check your mortgage statement, closing documents, or ask the servicer directly.
  2. Ask for the loss-mitigation department. Do not rely only on general customer service.
  3. Request the FHA assistance package. Ask where to upload documents and how to confirm receipt.
  4. Prepare income proof and hardship explanation. Keep the explanation factual and short.
  5. Track every document. Save upload confirmations, fax receipts, emails, and mail tracking numbers.
  6. Ask for written decision reasons. If denied, you need to know whether the problem was missing documents, income, eligibility, or investor rules.

Questions to ask your FHA servicer

  • What is my current delinquency amount and reinstatement amount?
  • What FHA loss-mitigation options are available for my account?
  • Have you reviewed my account for Payment Supplement?
  • Would a repayment plan require my normal payment plus an extra amount?
  • Would a modification increase the loan term or change the rate?
  • Would a partial claim be due when I sell, refinance, or pay off the loan?
  • What documents are missing from my file?
  • What is the deadline for submitting a complete package?
  • Will I need to complete a trial payment plan before a permanent modification?
  • If denied, is there an appeal or reconsideration process?

If your FHA modification request is denied

A denial does not always mean there are no options. It may mean documents were incomplete, income was not verified, a deadline passed, or one option did not fit. Ask for the denial reason in writing and ask whether another FHA retention option was reviewed.

If you believe the servicer made an error or failed to evaluate you properly, contact a HUD-approved housing counselor. They can help organize the file and may help you communicate more effectively with the servicer. If foreclosure has already started or legal papers have arrived, ask about legal aid in your state.

FHA rules are detailed, and timelines matter. The safest approach is to build a paper trail early and never wait until a sale date is close.

FAQ

Can FHA loans be modified?

Yes, FHA-insured loans may be eligible for FHA loss-mitigation options, including loan modification, partial claim, combination options, and Payment Supplement depending on current HUD rules and borrower eligibility.

Is an FHA partial claim free money?

No. A partial claim usually creates a subordinate obligation that may become due later, often when the home is sold, refinanced, or the first mortgage is paid off.

Who do I call for FHA mortgage help?

Call your mortgage servicer first and ask for the loss-mitigation department. You can also contact a HUD-approved housing counseling agency for help understanding your options.
