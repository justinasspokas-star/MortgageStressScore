Georgia homeowner guide

Georgia mortgage assistance in 2026
what to do before foreclosure moves fast.

Georgia homeowners behind on mortgage payments should act early. State grant funding may be limited or closed, but servicer loss mitigation, HUD counseling, disaster-related help, and legal advice can still matter before a foreclosure sale date.

Quick answer

The main Georgia Mortgage Assistance program is no longer accepting new applications as of March 1, 2026 according to the Georgia Department of Community Affairs. That does not mean you have no options. Call your mortgage servicer, ask for loss mitigation, check whether disaster-specific programs apply, and contact a HUD-approved housing counselor immediately.

Start here: your 3 moves this week

  1. Call your servicer’s loss-mitigation department. Ask what options are available for your loan type and request deadlines in writing.
  2. Contact a HUD-approved housing counselor. A counselor can review your budget, help organize documents, and prepare you for servicer calls.
  3. Check Georgia program status. Verify DCA/Georgia Mortgage Assistance status and whether any disaster-specific assistance applies to your county or hardship.

Georgia Mortgage Assistance status in 2026

The Georgia Department of Community Affairs says Georgia Mortgage Assistance is no longer accepting new applications as of March 1, 2026 because of high application volume and limited available funding. If you see older articles saying the program is open, verify directly on the official DCA or Georgia Mortgage Assistance site before relying on it.

Practical meaning: do not wait for a state grant before contacting your servicer. Your servicer’s loss-mitigation review is usually the fastest path to options like repayment plan, forbearance, deferral/partial claim, or loan modification.

Georgia foreclosure timeline: why speed matters

Georgia generally uses a nonjudicial foreclosure process. The Georgia Attorney General explains that Georgia law allows lenders to conduct an auction without first going before a judge if the lender gives proper legal notice. The Georgia Attorney General also states that notice must be sent at least 30 days before the proposed foreclosure sale.

Georgia options to check

Servicer loss mitigation

Ask about repayment plan, forbearance, deferral or partial claim if available, loan modification, and disaster assistance if your hardship relates to a declared event.

HUD-approved counseling

HUD-approved housing counselors can help you organize paperwork and understand options before foreclosure deadlines tighten.

Georgia DCA / GMA status

Check the official Georgia Department of Community Affairs page and Georgia Mortgage Assistance portal for current status, closed programs, or special disaster-related assistance.

Legal aid

If you receive a foreclosure notice or sale date, ask a legal aid organization or qualified attorney about your rights and deadlines under Georgia law.

Script to call your servicer

Say this on the call

“I live in Georgia and I am having trouble with my mortgage payment. I want to be reviewed for all available loss-mitigation options for my loan type. Please tell me what documents are needed, whether my application will be considered complete, what deadline applies, and whether any foreclosure activity or sale date is scheduled. Please send the document list and deadlines in writing.”

Documents to prepare

  • Mortgage statement and servicer account number
  • Most recent pay stubs, benefit letters, unemployment proof, or self-employment income records
  • Bank statements requested by the servicer
  • Hardship letter explaining what changed and when
  • Escrow analysis, tax bill, insurance increase, medical bills, or disaster repair estimates if relevant
  • Written log of all servicer calls, upload confirmations, and mailed documents

Avoid Georgia mortgage relief scams

Do not pay upfront for promises

FTC warns that companies asking for upfront fees or guaranteeing mortgage relief are a major scam risk. If someone says they can stop a Georgia foreclosure without reviewing your loan documents, servicer notices, and deadlines, slow down and verify.

Next step

If you have no sale notice, use that time to submit a complete loss-mitigation package. If you already received a Georgia foreclosure notice, do not wait: call the servicer, contact a HUD counselor, and speak with legal aid or an attorney about deadlines.
