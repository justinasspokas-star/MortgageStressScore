New Jersey mortgage assistance: ERMA status and free help

New Jersey's ERMA program is no longer accepting new applications. If you need mortgage help today, ask your servicer about hardship options and use free counseling through NJHMFA's FMAP program or a HUD-approved housing counselor.

Last reviewed: October 19, 2026 · Editorial policy

Mortgage assistance options in New Jersey

  • ERMA (Emergency Rescue Mortgage Assistance): Closed to new applications as of this review. Prior funding limits are historical, not a current offer. Check NJHMFA status.
  • FMAP counseling: NJHMFA's Foreclosure Mediation Assistance Program offers free pre-foreclosure counseling and assistance with mediation for eligible homeowners. Official FMAP information.
  • Servicer hardship review: Contact the company collecting your mortgage payment and ask about forbearance, repayment plans, modifications and options specific to FHA, VA, USDA or conventional loans.
  • Legal Services of New Jersey: Income-eligible homeowners facing foreclosure can seek legal assistance through LSNJ or 1-888-576-5529.

How the free FMAP program works

FMAP supports homeowners who are already behind or at risk of missing a payment. Participating counseling agencies can help you organize financial information, review servicer options and, when eligible, prepare for New Jersey Judiciary foreclosure mediation. Counseling is available even before a foreclosure complaint is filed. Check the NJHMFA foreclosure-prevention page for participating agencies.

What to do first if you cannot pay your mortgage

  1. Call your mortgage servicer as early as possible. Explain the hardship and request a written checklist of loss-mitigation options and required documents.
  2. Contact a free counselor. A HUD-approved housing counselor can help review alternatives; see CFPB mortgage help.
  3. Keep records. Record dates, reference numbers, messages, document submissions and deadlines.
  4. Act promptly if legal papers arrive. New Jersey foreclosure is a court process; deadlines can be short. Seek an attorney or legal aid, not a generic timeline estimate.

Foreclosure and mediation deadlines in New Jersey

New Jersey uses judicial foreclosure for most residential mortgages. The time from a complaint to a sheriff's sale varies substantially; do not assume a fixed 2–4-year window. Read any complaint or sale notice immediately and consult New Jersey Courts.

Mediation deadline: NJHMFA states that eligible homeowners generally must request New Jersey Judiciary foreclosure mediation within 60 days of service of the summons and complaint, unless a court orders otherwise. Separate response deadlines may apply; ask an attorney about your case.

Property tax programs are not emergency mortgage grants

The former New Jersey Homestead Benefit program was replaced by ANCHOR. Depending on the rules in effect and your eligibility, ANCHOR or Senior Freeze may help with property taxes, but they do not replace mortgage arrears assistance. Visit the NJ Division of Taxation for current details and filing windows.

Frequently asked questions

Is ERMA taking applications in October 2026?

No. The official NJHMFA ERMA page says it is no longer accepting applications. Check directly with NJHMFA if you already applied.

Can I get free mortgage help before foreclosure?

Yes. FMAP offers pre-foreclosure counseling and HUD-approved counselors provide free foreclosure-prevention assistance.

Does the free Mortgage Stress Score qualify me for a grant?

No. It is an educational estimate, not a lender decision, financial advice or government assistance application.
