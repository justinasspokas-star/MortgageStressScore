Methodology

How the Mortgage Stress Score
is estimated.

MortgageStressScore is a private, browser-based educational tool. It uses six homeowner inputs to estimate payment stress, urgency, and practical next steps. It is designed to help people organize what to do next — not to replace a servicer, counselor, attorney, lender, or credit model.

Last reviewed: July 2026Educational estimate onlyNo phone number required

Plain-English summary: the score looks at mortgage payment burden, missed payments, equity flexibility, loan type, and urgency. Higher scores mean the homeowner should act faster, document more carefully, and consider contacting the mortgage servicer and a HUD-approved housing counselor.

Purpose of the score

The calculator was built for homeowners who are asking urgent questions such as “Can I still fix this?”, “Should I call my servicer?”, “Is foreclosure risk increasing?”, or “Which mortgage relief option should I learn about first?”

The score is not meant to predict an exact legal result. Mortgage outcomes depend on the loan owner, servicer procedures, state law, investor rules, documentation, hardship type, payment history, and timing. A simple online calculator cannot know all of that. The goal is to give a fast, conservative signal that helps a homeowner decide whether to prepare, call, or escalate.

Because mortgage hardship is a high-impact financial topic, the site intentionally points users toward their mortgage servicer and HUD-approved housing counseling resources before suggesting any paid resource.

How risk levels are interpreted

The calculator produces a number from 0 to 100. It then translates that number into a practical risk level. The thresholds are intentionally simple so the result is easy to understand during a stressful moment.

Score rangeRisk labelMeaningRecommended direction
0–34Low stressThe mortgage appears manageable based on the inputs, but planning still matters.Build a payment buffer, review loan terms annually, and keep servicer contact information handy.
35–64Moderate pressureThe payment, loan type, or anxiety level suggests the homeowner should act before the situation escalates.Stress-test the next 90 days, gather documents, and ask the servicer about options before missing payments.
65–100High riskThe inputs suggest urgency, especially if payments are already missed.Contact the servicer and a HUD-approved housing counselor quickly. Avoid upfront-fee rescue offers.

What the score does not use

MortgageStressScore does not access a credit report, bank account, tax record, property title, automated valuation model, lender file, or servicer system. It does not know whether an investor will approve forbearance, repayment, deferral, modification, refinance, short sale, or any other option.

The tool also does not ask for a Social Security number, phone number, account login, or full address. That is intentional. The product is designed as a private first step for people who may not be ready to speak to a company yet.

How official guidance shapes the recommendations

The score recommendations are based around conservative, consumer-protection-first guidance. For example, homeowners who are behind or close to missing payments are directed to contact their mortgage servicer and consider a HUD-approved housing counselor. CFPB and HUD both provide official consumer resources for avoiding foreclosure and finding housing counseling help.

The site also includes scam warnings because the FTC warns that mortgage relief scams commonly target homeowners in distress and that upfront-fee promises are a major red flag.

Important limitations

  • Not a credit score: it does not predict FICO, VantageScore, underwriting, or credit approval.
  • Not legal advice: foreclosure procedures and timelines vary by state and by case.
  • Not financial advice: it does not recommend a specific loan, product, attorney, servicer strategy, or debt plan.
  • Not an eligibility decision: only a servicer, program administrator, lender, agency, or qualified counselor can evaluate actual eligibility.
  • Not emergency assistance: if a foreclosure sale date, court deadline, or notice has arrived, users should seek qualified local help immediately.

How the methodology is reviewed

The methodology is reviewed when mortgage servicing rules, FHA loss-mitigation guidance, consumer-protection warnings, or site data suggest that recommendations need adjustment. Pages that discuss official programs should be rechecked regularly because government and servicer programs can change.

When the calculator logic changes materially, this page should be updated so users can understand the new version. Minor wording changes may be made without changing the core scoring logic.
